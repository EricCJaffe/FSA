## Why Lower-Priced LTRs Are AI-Resilient

The defining macro question for 2026-2031 is what happens to housing if AI displaces white-collar workers. The emerging consensus from Redfin, Citrini Research, and mortgage industry analysts points to a structural shift:

1. **White-collar AI displacement is concentrated in the $80K-$200K income brackets.** Estimates range from 200K-300K displaced in 2025, potentially 10-15M over five years as adoption accelerates. Block, Salesforce, Meta, and Microsoft have all announced AI-driven headcount reductions.

2. **Displaced workers move DOWN the housing chain** — they become renters, not buyers. Apartment demand is projected to rise 2-3% in 2026 with supply already constrained.

3. **Our rent range ($1,100-$1,300) maps to ~$45K-$55K household income** — blue collar, trades, service industry. These tenants are not the displaced white-collar group; in many cases they are the *destination* for the displaced group who can no longer afford ownership.

4. **Top 10% of earners drive 50%+ of spending.** Their spending cuts hurt LUXURY first (coastal, condos, high-end single family) — not basics like working-class rental housing.

5. **Redfin 2026 predictions explicitly** cite apartment demand rising as unemployment hits white-collar buyers.

## Risk Factors (Ranked by Severity)

1. **Florida Insurance.** Premiums up 40-80% since 2022; ongoing annual increases expected. Model assumes 8% annual insurance inflation going forward. This is the #1 risk to long-term Florida RE returns.

2. **Hurricane Concentration.** All five properties within 30 miles. One major storm could damage multiple simultaneously. Insurance covers but gaps are real (hurricane deductibles 2-5% of coverage = potentially $20K+ per property).

3. **AI Economic Shock.** If white-collar unemployment spikes above 6%, even LTR rents could soften temporarily. Our thesis expects lower-end rentals to be insulated, but this is the single most important macro variable to watch.

4. **Florida Migration Slowdown.** Net domestic migration to Florida dropped from 310K (2022) to 22K (2025) — 93% collapse. Drivers: insurance costs, HOA fees, affordability erosion. If migration turns negative, FL RE appreciation assumptions need revision.

5. **Interest Rates.** Rates above 6.5% choke home sales and suppress future appreciation. Freddie Mac shows 30-year at 6.14% (Feb 2026). Fed projections imply modest easing in 2026.

6. **Concentration.** 100% of wealth in 5 properties within 30 miles. Zero public market exposure. Zero cash buffer. One of the strongest arguments for Scenarios 3 or 6.
