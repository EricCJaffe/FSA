# Decision Framework — Mission-Weighted Scoring

## Methodology

Each scenario is scored 1-5 on eight criteria, then weighted by mission priority. This is not pure dollar-optimization; it reflects Eric and Mary Jo's stated priority matrix.

## The Close Call

Scenarios 3 and 6 are effectively tied (4.35 vs 4.30). This is within margin of error. The tiebreaker is **optionality**:

- **Scenario 3 is irreversible.** Once Scenic is sold, it's gone.
- **Scenario 6 is reversible.** Can pivot to Scenario 3 at any future checkpoint.

When two paths score essentially the same, choose the one that preserves the ability to change course.

## Recommendation: Scenario 6 for 24 Months, Then Reassess

### Why Scenario 6 First

- Highest 5-year total net worth ($1.77M)
- Preserves waterfront optionality
- Begins structural diversification (S&P stack)
- Tests the LTR thesis with real-world data
- Reversible at every step
- Mary Jo retains influence (vs. irreversible sale)

### Why Reassess at 24 Months

By April 2028:
- LTR Scenic will have 2 years of actual performance data
- Will know if $3K/mo rent holds or softens
- Will have ~$90K S&P position (some market cycle data)
- Jacksonville market trajectory will be clearer
- AI economic impact on job market will be more visible
- Mary Jo will have processed the Scenic vacation transition

At that point, a fresh Sell vs. Keep decision can be made with actual evidence rather than projections.

## Pivot Triggers (When to Move Faster)

Consider accelerating to Scenario 3 before 24 months if:
- LTR tenant placement fails or rent comes in materially below $3K/mo
- Scenic experiences major repair need ($50K+) that erodes economic thesis
- Hurricane damages property — consider sale from insurance + cleanup proceeds
- Florida insurance environment deteriorates significantly
- S&P market experiences major drawdown (accelerate buying opportunity)

## Pivot Triggers (When to Slow Down)

Consider staying in Scenario 6 longer than 24 months if:
- LTR performs exceptionally well
- Scenic waterfront comps appreciate materially (>5%/yr)
- Mary Jo wants family vacation use back (revert to limited personal + tenant hybrid if possible)
- S&P valuations look overextended
- You need to reduce operational complexity during a life-season transition

## Four-Step Implementation Plan (if proceeding with Scenario 6)

### Step 1 — Validate (Days 0-30)
- Shoreward confirms $3K/mo LTR rent with recent comparable leases
- Obtain LTR-specific insurance quotes (2-3 carriers)
- Confirm depreciation figures with CPA for future sale tax planning
- Schedule final family vacation(s) at Scenic for emotional closure

### Step 2 — Convert (Days 30-90)
- Stop accepting new STR bookings
- List Scenic as LTR via Shoreward
- Thorough tenant screening
- Sign 12-month lease with renewal option
- Switch insurance to LTR policy

### Step 3 — Stack (Days 90-180)
- Open Vanguard or Fidelity taxable brokerage
- Set up automated monthly investment of ~$3,800/mo
- Build $50K HYSA emergency reserve (from Shoreward accrual + first 3-4 months CF)
- Decide on allocation: VTI / VTSAX for US, optionally add VXUS for international

### Step 4 — Alternative Vacation Strategy (Days 90+)
- Budget ~$7,500/yr for 3 weeks of vacation rentals
- Rotate destinations (beach, mountain, international)
- Book intentional family trip as the "goodbye to Scenic" reset

## Review Cadence

- **Monthly (15 min):** Cash flow reconciliation. Shoreward → bank → S&P auto-invest executing correctly.
- **Quarterly (1 hour):** Portfolio review vs this document. Thesis still holding?
- **Annually (half-day):** Full re-analysis with Mary Jo.
- **24-month checkpoint:** Formal decision — continue Scenario 6 or pivot to Scenario 3.
