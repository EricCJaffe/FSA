# AI Economic Stress Test

## Downturn Scenario Assumptions

Modeled as an AI-driven economic disruption over 3-5 years:

- White-collar unemployment rises from 3.5% to 8% by Year 3 (10-15M displaced)
- S&P 500 drawdown of 20-30% peak-to-trough; recovery by Year 3-4
- Florida coastal housing: down 5-15%
- Jax Beach / coastal condos: down 10-20%
- SFH sub-$250K: flat to +1%/yr (supply-constrained, rental demand strong)
- $500K+ SFH: down 5-10%
- STR revenue: down 20-25% (discretionary travel cut first)
- Mountain STR values: down 5-10%
- Mortgage delinquency rate: up from 2.4% to potentially 8.5% if job losses accelerate

## Property-by-Property Expected Behavior

### 4 LTRs (Fremont / Sunderland / Solar B / Solar C) — RESILIENT

- Rent demand likely *increases* as displaced workers downsize from ownership to rental
- Rent growth may slow (from 2.5% base to 1-1.5%) but occupancy stays strong
- Cash flow remains positive throughout
- Property values flat to slightly negative, but this is irrelevant if held long-term
- **Thesis validated:** lower-priced SFH rentals are the AI-insulated asset class

### Scenic as STR — HURT

- Discretionary travel cut first in recession
- Revenue down 15-25% from already-weak baseline
- Losses deepen from ~$1,700 to potentially $5,000-7,000/yr
- If kept as STR through downturn, becomes a meaningful drag

### Scenic as LTR — INSULATED

- Stable long-term tenant, insulated from travel cycle
- Rent may not grow during downturn but shouldn't decline (these aren't luxury rents)
- Potentially one of the strongest portfolio positions in downturn
- Converts what was a weakness into a strength

### Scenic as Sale Proceeds — MIXED

- If already sold before downturn: fine, proceeds in S&P or cash
- If forced to sell DURING downturn: 5-8% value haircut on ~$500K = $25-40K
- Timing risk is real if you're late to decide

### Jacksonville Beach Condo (Scenario 4) — WORST

- Coastal + condo + luxury segment = triple exposure
- Value drop 10-20%
- HOA assessments likely accelerate (SB 4-D + insurance claims)
- Already had negative cash flow; becomes more negative
- Forced sale during downturn could mean 15-25% loss

### Mountain STR (Scenario 5) — HURT

- Revenue down 20% in recession (travel cut)
- Value down 5-10%
- Already marginal cash flow becomes negative
- Active management burden increases at worst time

### S&P 500 Position — DRAWDOWN THEN RECOVERY

- Down 25% by trough (if severe recession)
- Recovered +30% net over 3-4 years typically
- **Scenario 6 benefits uniquely** from buying during drawdown via auto-invest (dollar-cost averaging into lower prices)

## Stress Test Ranking

## Key Insight for Decision-Making

Scenarios 2 and 6 (both keep Scenic as LTR) remain standing in virtually every downturn model. The blue-collar LTR base is the single most resilient asset in the portfolio.

Scenario 4 (Beach House) is the most fragile. Do not choose it expecting financial return — only as a pure lifestyle asset you intend to hold regardless of market outcomes.

## What Changes the Thesis

The working-class LTR resilience thesis weakens if:
- Unemployment rises to Depression-era levels (>15%)
- Government responds with housing disruption (e.g., eviction moratoriums, rent freezes)
- Florida population exodus accelerates (already declining net migration)
- Major hurricane damages multiple properties simultaneously
- Insurance becomes unavailable or prohibitively expensive

If 2-3 of these trigger simultaneously, the analysis needs a full refresh.
