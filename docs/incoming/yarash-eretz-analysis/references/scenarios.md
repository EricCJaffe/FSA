# Six Scenarios — Detailed Analysis

All scenarios retain the 4 Jacksonville/Orange Park LTRs. The variable in every scenario is what happens to Scenic Drive and where (if anywhere) the proceeds or excess cash go.

Base case assumptions: Jacksonville appreciation 2.5%/yr, Scenic waterfront 3%/yr, rent growth 2.5%/yr, S&P 500 8.5%/yr, Florida insurance inflation 8%/yr.

## Scenario 1 — Status Quo

**What happens:** Keep everything exactly as-is. Scenic continues as STR.

**Strengths:**
- No new decisions, no new tax consequences, no new debt
- Preserves family vacation use of Scenic (4 weeks/year, ~$10K value)
- Lowest-change option

**Weaknesses:**
- Scenic keeps bleeding ~$1,700/yr cash
- No progress toward diversification
- Misses the opportunity cost of $500K tied up in a non-performing asset
- Dead last on 5-year cash flow among all scenarios

**5-year total net worth:** $1,346,469 (+$257K)
**Mission rank:** 4th

## Scenario 2 — LTR Convert

**What happens:** Keep all five properties. Convert Scenic from STR to LTR at $3,000/month (Shoreward estimate).

**Strengths:**
- Transforms Scenic from -$1,700 to +$17,068 annual cash flow (+$18,810 swing)
- Preserves waterfront asset and appreciation upside
- Eliminates STR management burden (no check-ins, cleaning, guest issues)
- No tax consequences (not selling)
- Insurance drops ~30% (LTR rider vs STR)
- Reversible — can convert back to STR or sell later

**Weaknesses:**
- Loses personal vacation use of Scenic (~$10K/yr lifestyle value)
- Builds no public-market diversification
- Excess cash just accumulates (doesn't compound)

**5-year total net worth:** $1,513,293
**Mission rank:** 3rd

## Scenario 3 — Sell + S&P 500

**What happens:** Sell Scenic (~$441K net proceeds after taxes/costs). Deploy proceeds to S&P 500 index funds.

**Strengths:**
- Highest mission-weighted score (4.35)
- Best 20-year outcome due to S&P compounding ($4.09M)
- Immediate, complete diversification of the $500K position
- Liquidity dramatically improves
- Full passivity — no management

**Weaknesses:**
- **Irreversible** — waterfront asset gone forever
- Loses all personal vacation use
- Tax hit at sale: ~$10.5K cap gains + $18.6K depreciation recapture
- S&P drawdown risk in near-term downturn

**5-year total net worth:** $1,507,297
**10-year:** $2,133,425
**20-year:** $4,086,388 (WINNER long-term)
**Mission rank:** 1st

## Scenario 4 — Sell + Buy Beach House/Condo

**What happens:** Sell Scenic. Buy $700K Jacksonville Beach property with $300K down, financing $400K at ~6.25%.

**Strengths:**
- Beach lifestyle asset
- Preserves vacation venue (in a different form)

**Weaknesses:**
- **Negative cash flow: -$42,316/yr** driven by mortgage + HOA + coastal insurance
- Jax Beach condo segment faces SB 4-D structural headwinds
- Jax Beach is more hurricane-exposed than Middleburg
- Worst 5-year net worth outcome ($1.32M)

**Verdict:** Only rational if bought as pure lifestyle asset regardless of financial outcome. Don't pretend it's an "investment."

**Mission rank:** 5th

## Scenario 5 — Sell + Buy Mountain STR

**What happens:** Sell Scenic. Buy $600K Smokies cabin with $250K down. Operate as STR.

**Strengths:**
- Diversifies geography (out of Florida insurance pressure)
- Tennessee STR-friendly regulation
- Modest positive cash flow projected ($19K/yr)

**Weaknesses:**
- Trades one STR problem for another
- STR revenue hit 20-25% in any recession
- Active management complexity (guest relations, pricing, cleaning coordination)
- Supply correction already hit mountain STR markets 2024-2025
- Mission rank dead last (6th) — adds complexity without clear strategic win

## Scenario 6 — LTR Convert + S&P 500 Stack (RECOMMENDED)

**What happens:** Execute Scenario 2 (LTR convert) PLUS direct the now-positive annual cash flow (~$46K/yr) into S&P 500 index funds via automated monthly contributions.

**Strengths:**
- Highest 5-year net worth of all scenarios: **$1,774,606**
- Keeps all waterfront optionality
- Begins structural diversification without severing RE exposure
- AI/recession resilient (diversified income + equity)
- Reversible at every step
- At 24-month checkpoint, can pivot to Scenario 3 with fresh data

**Weaknesses:**
- Loses Scenic vacation use (like Scenario 2)
- Mission score marginally behind Scenario 3 (4.30 vs 4.35) — essentially a tie
- 20-year outcome slightly behind Scenario 3 ($3.91M vs $4.09M)

**Why this is the recommendation over Scenario 3:**
- Mission score delta is within margin of error
- Scenarios 2 and 6 are *reversible*; 3 is not
- Waterfront properties are unique and hard to re-enter
- LTR @ $3K/mo is a Shoreward *estimate* — needs real-world validation
- Mary Jo has a vote, and 24 months gives time to honor that

**5-year:** $1,774,606
**10-year:** $2,232,642
**20-year:** $3,908,062
**Mission rank:** 2nd

## The Honest Trade-off

**Scenario 3** wins on math. **Scenario 6** wins on optionality. They converge around year 24-25 if everything goes to plan.

For an irreversible decision affecting a unique asset that's tied to family memory (Scenic), optionality is worth the small expected-value cost. Revisit at the 24-month checkpoint.
